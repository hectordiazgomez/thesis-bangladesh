\begin{abstract}
\noindent This paper studies how Bangladesh's nationwide Internet shutdown of July 2024 affected observable software-development activity. Using public GitHub events for 59,158 pre-active accounts associated with Bangladesh and fourteen comparison countries, I measure the daily share of eligible users with productive GitHub activity. During the July 18--23 shutdown, this share averaged 2.02 percent in Bangladesh, compared with 15.28 percent for a synthetic control: an estimated effect of $-$13.26 percentage points ($-$86.8 percent). An international difference-in-differences comparison yields $-$13.09 percentage points. Bangladesh is the most extreme of fifteen units in an in-space placebo test, although with fourteen placebo countries the exact rank $p$-value cannot fall below 0.0667. Git metadata show that 829 of the 2,932 commits authored during July 18--23 (28.3 percent) were first pushed after restoration. In levels, however, the backlog is small. For the cohort authored after the nationwide cutoff at about 15:00 UTC on July 18, creation of code observed publicly by August 10 fell 96.35 percent relative to duration-matched pre-shutdown windows, and this cohort's delayed commits equal 2.50 percent of normal volume; code pushed later or never published is unobserved. Against the synthetic series, the shutdown deficit is 8,145 active user-days and is only partly offset by September 4. An international extension estimates separate synthetic controls for fixed pre-treatment repository-domain cohorts, with explicit classification coverage, sample-support rules and placebo diagnostics. Forty eligible accounts publish during the interior shutdown days; an individual case audit finds direct automation evidence in 113 of their 180 pushes, qualifying an interpretation of residual activity as continued human coding. Within-Bangladesh recovery patterns are retained as descriptive appendix evidence. All outcomes measure public GitHub activity, not total software productivity. Because the shutdown coincided with severe domestic unrest, the estimates are interpreted as effects of the shutdown episode, assuming that no other Bangladesh-specific shock produced the same abrupt break.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Internet shutdowns; software production; GitHub; synthetic control; difference-in-differences; digital economy.
\end{abstract}
