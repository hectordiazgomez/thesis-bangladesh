\begin{abstract}
\noindent This paper studies how Bangladesh's nationwide Internet shutdown of July 2024 affected observable software-development activity. Using public GitHub events for 59,158 pre-active accounts associated with Bangladesh and fourteen comparison countries, I measure the daily share of eligible users with productive GitHub activity. The paper makes three contributions. First, it documents an immediate collapse. During the July 18--23 shutdown, this share averaged 2.02 percent in Bangladesh, compared with 15.28 percent for a synthetic control, an estimated effect of $-$13.26 percentage points ($-$86.8 percent). An international difference-in-differences comparison yields $-$13.09 percentage points, and Bangladesh is the most extreme unit in an in-space placebo test, with an exact rank $p$-value of 0.0667, the minimum attainable with fourteen placebo countries. Second, Git metadata reveal a small delayed-publication backlog alongside a collapse in observable author-time creation. After the approximately 15:00 UTC July 18 onset, creation of code subsequently observed publicly by August 10 falls roughly 96--97 percent relative to pre-shutdown comparison windows; backlog is about 2.5 percent of normal volume. The original calendar-cohort backlog share of 28.3 percent therefore does not imply extensive continuation of work. Third, restoring connectivity did not imply a uniform recovery. In a fixed sample of 20,000 Bangladeshi accounts, activity in repositories associated with finance and information recovered fastest, whereas activity associated with accommodation and food remained depressed throughout the observed window. These sectoral patterns are descriptive comparisons with each sector's own baseline, not sector-specific causal effects. All outcomes measure observable public GitHub activity rather than total software productivity. Because the shutdown coincided with severe domestic unrest, the estimates are interpreted as effects of the shutdown episode under the assumption that no other Bangladesh-specific shock produced the same abrupt break.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} Internet shutdowns; software production; GitHub; synthetic control; difference-in-differences; digital economy.
\end{abstract}
