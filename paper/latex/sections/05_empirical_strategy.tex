\section{Empirical Strategy}
\label{sec:strategy}

\subsection{Synthetic control}
\label{sec:sc}

The headline design treats Bangladesh as the single treated country and the fourteen other countries as the donor pool. Let $Y_{ct}$ denote the daily active-user rate for country $c$ on date $t$. Synthetic-control weights are nonnegative, sum to one, and are chosen to minimize the mean squared difference between Bangladesh and the weighted donor combination over the pre-treatment period May 1--July 17. The estimated daily treatment effect is the gap between Bangladesh and the synthetic control during July 18--23.

The method suits the setting because treatment occurs at the country level and the number of aggregate comparison units is modest. Its main advantage here is transparency: the counterfactual is an explicit weighted combination of observed countries, and the credibility of the design can be evaluated through pre-treatment fit, donor weights, in-space placebos, and leave-one-out exercises \citep*{abadie2010,abadie2021}. Writing $w_j$ for the weight on donor $j$, the estimated average treatment effect on the treated over the shutdown is
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{ATT}_{\mathrm{SC}} = \frac{1}{|\mathcal{T}_{1}|}\sum_{t \in \mathcal{T}_{1}}
  \Bigl[\, Y_{\mathrm{BD},t} - \sum_{j} w_{j} Y_{j,t} \Bigr],
  \label{eq:att_sc}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{T}_{1}$ denotes the shutdown days.

\paragraph{How the weights are obtained.} Let $\mathcal T_0$ be the 78 dates from May 1 to July 17, and let $\Delta^{14}=\{w\in\mathbb R^{14}:w_j\geq0,\sum_{j=1}^{14}w_j=1\}$. The estimator solves
\begin{equation}
 \widehat w=\arg\min_{w\in\Delta^{14}}\frac{1}{78}\sum_{t\in\mathcal T_0}
 \left(Y_{\mathrm{BD},t}-\sum_{j=1}^{14}w_jY_{jt}\right)^2.
 \label{eq:sc_minimization}
\end{equation}
Thus every pre-shutdown daily outcome is a lagged predictor. If $X_1$ is Bangladesh's $78\times1$ vector and $X_0$ the $78\times14$ donor matrix, the objective is $(X_1-X_0w)'V(X_1-X_0w)$ with $V=I_{78}/78$. No intercept or extrapolation beyond the donors' convex hull is permitted. The optimization uses only pre-treatment observations; the six shutdown observations do not select donors or tune weights. The three positive weights are solutions to this constrained least-squares problem, rather than subjective measures of economic similarity. A zero weight does not mean a country was dropped from the original donor pool. Numerically, nonnegative least squares uses a heavily weighted adding-up constraint, followed by normalization and verification that weights sum to one. The reported fit is evaluated in percentage points on the original daily series.

\paragraph{Covariates and identifying assumptions.} The primary specification matches lagged outcomes only. It includes no temperature, precipitation, electricity-use or macroeconomic covariates, and it does not estimate a separate predictor-weighting matrix. Historical outcomes may summarize persistent determinants of activity, but this does not guarantee balance on every relevant factor. To assess dependence on the lag representation, I also report fits using weekly means, the last 28 or 14 daily observations, and weekday means; these are sensitivity exercises, not replacements selected by their treatment estimates. Pre-treatment holdout errors are reported alongside them. Electricity consumption measured during the shutdown could itself respond to the disruption and must not be added as an ordinary control for its total effect. Weather controls would require a geographically defensible measure of developers' exposure; assigning national developers the weather of one capital city would introduce an unvalidated proxy. Neither is silently included in the estimates.

For $\widehat Y^0_{\mathrm{BD},t}=\sum_j\widehat w_jY_{jt}$ to approximate Bangladesh without the episode, the pre-period relationship must remain stable, donors must not be affected by Bangladesh's shutdown or their own coincident interventions, and no other Bangladesh-specific shock may generate the same discontinuity. International collaboration can transmit spillovers, while domestic unrest can violate the last assumption. Good pre-treatment fit is a diagnostic for these conditions, not proof of them. Placebos re-estimate the weights for each comparison country; the small donor pool limits the attainable rank probabilities. The main estimate is therefore conditional on these assumptions and concerns observable public activity during the shutdown episode.

\subsection{Difference-in-differences and event study}
\label{sec:did}

As a complementary design, I compare the change in Bangladesh's mean outcome from the pre-period to the shutdown with the average corresponding change across the donor countries:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{ATT}_{\mathrm{DiD}} = \bigl[\bar{Y}_{\mathrm{BD},1} - \bar{Y}_{\mathrm{BD},0}\bigr]
  - \frac{1}{14}\sum_{j=1}^{14}\bigl[\bar{Y}_{j,1} - \bar{Y}_{j,0}\bigr],
  \label{eq:did_agg}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{Y}_{c,0}$ is country $c$'s mean daily active-user rate over May 1--July 17 and $\bar{Y}_{c,1}$ its mean over July 18--23. Unlike the synthetic control, which chooses donor weights to reproduce Bangladesh before the shutdown, this comparison gives equal weight to every donor. It is used primarily as a magnitude check against the synthetic-control estimate.

To trace the dynamics of the gap, I replace the shutdown window with weekly event periods $k$ and compute
\begin{equation}
  \hat\theta_{k} = \bigl[\bar{Y}_{\mathrm{BD},k} - \bar{Y}_{\mathrm{BD},0}\bigr]
  - \frac{1}{14}\sum_{j=1}^{14}\bigl[\bar{Y}_{j,k} - \bar{Y}_{j,0}\bigr],
  \label{eq:es_agg}
\end{equation}
where $\bar{Y}_{c,k}$ is country $c$'s mean daily active-user rate in event period $k$; $k=-11,\dots,-1$ are seven-day bins ending July 17 (bin $-11$ also contains May 1), $k=0$ is the shutdown (July 18--23), and $k=+1,\dots,+6$ are seven-day bins from July 24 to September 3. \Cref{eq:es_agg} is computed directly from the stored country-day panel of the fifteen countries; it involves no regression and no user-level fixed effects. By construction, $\hat\theta_0 = \mathrm{ATT}_{\mathrm{DiD}}$, so the shutdown-week coefficient reproduces the aggregate DiD magnitude. Under parallel pre-shutdown paths, the coefficients for $k<0$ would fluctuate narrowly around zero. As shown in \cref{sec:did_results}, Bangladesh's pre-period is volatile and does not display a clean conventional parallel-trends pattern. The weekly coefficients are therefore not interpreted as an identified dynamic causal treatment path. They are diagnostic evidence on whether the largest break in the series is concentrated at the shutdown, and they complement, rather than replace, the synthetic control.

Because treatment varies only at the country level and there are fifteen countries, I do not rely on user-clustered standard errors. Uncertainty is instead summarized with in-space placebos: each donor is assigned the treatment in turn and compared with the equal-weighted mean of the remaining thirteen donors. I report the standard deviation of the fourteen placebo estimates, a donor-placebo reference interval equal to $\hat\theta_k \pm 1.96$ times that standard deviation, and the rank of Bangladesh's absolute estimate among the fifteen units. These reference intervals describe how far donor countries move under the same calculation; they are not conventional sampling confidence intervals, and because Bangladesh's pre-period is more volatile than any donor's, they are not fully calibrated measures of uncertainty for Bangladesh.

\paragraph{Temporal robustness.} Regression discontinuity in time is retained only as a sensitivity check. Its cutoff predates the nationwide outage, and its estimates depend on bandwidth and sample; the equation and complete results are reported in \cref{app:rdit_detail}.

\subsection{Inference and robustness}
\label{sec:inference}

For synthetic control, I implement in-space placebos by assigning the treatment date to each donor country in turn and reconstructing its synthetic control using the remaining donors. The primary rank statistic compares Bangladesh's absolute ATT and post/pre RMSPE ratio with the placebo distribution. \citet*{abadie2015} illustrate the use of post/pre RMSPE ratios to scale post-intervention discrepancies by pre-intervention fit, alongside in-time placebos that reassign treatment to earlier dates. With fourteen placebo countries, the smallest possible finite-sample rank value is $1/(14+1) = 0.0667$; consequently, a value of 0.0667 means Bangladesh is the most extreme unit, not that the effect is weak.

I also re-estimate the primary synthetic control after removing each donor country one at a time. Holidays are excluded in a separate DiD specification. Finally, outcome-specific synthetic controls allow donor weights to vary by outcome, whereas the main secondary-outcome table deliberately reuses the active-rate synthetic-control weights. Reusing the primary weights reduces the risk that each new outcome is matched with a different donor combination chosen after observing results.


\subsection{International synthetic controls by repository domain}
\label{sec:sector_sc_method}

Let $G_i$ be the domain assigned before treatment and $N_{cs}$ the fixed number of eligible users in country $c$ assigned to group $s$. The country-domain outcome is
\begin{equation}
 Y_{cst}=\frac{100}{N_{cs}}\sum_{i\in\mathcal U_c}\mathbf1\{G_i=s\}\mathbf1\{P_{it}>0\}.
 \label{eq:sector_cohort}
\end{equation}
It counts the same primary public activity anywhere on GitHub among this fixed cohort; it does not condition on remaining in the original repository after the shutdown. Post-treatment switching therefore cannot change membership or the denominator. Each country-domain series is compared with the same domain in other countries, solving \cref{eq:sc_minimization} separately for $s$, on the same 78 pre-treatment dates. The shutdown ATT is the six-day mean of $Y_{\mathrm{BD},st}-\sum_j\widehat w_{js}Y_{jst}$.

The support rule requires at least 30 eligible users in the Bangladesh group and in each candidate donor group, and at least five eligible donor countries. This rule is based on sample support, not the observed effect. Every supported domain is reported, together with its denominator, donor count, pre-fit, placebo ranks and leave-one-out sensitivity. Unsupported domains are listed separately; they are not assigned zero effects. The unclassified group is also estimated when supported. Confidence-threshold sensitivities change membership explicitly and never replace the original national sample. The July 1--17 holdout diagnostic refits weights through June 30; group definitions remain fixed at July 17, so this is a conditional fit diagnostic rather than a fully prospective prediction experiment.

A causal interpretation requires the same counterfactual stability and no-spillover conditions as national synthetic control, now within each group, as well as comparable measurement across countries. In particular, sector-specific unrest or differences in unobserved jobs may violate these conditions. Country placebos provide a reference distribution, with minimum rank value $1/(J_s+1)$ when $J_s$ donors qualify; they do not justify conventional 5-percent significance claims or a causal ranking across many sectors. Different group effects need not be statistically distinguishable. The observed national rate can be reconstructed exactly as $Y_{ct}=\sum_s(N_{cs}/N_c)Y_{cst}$, including the unclassified group. The sector-specific synthetic counterfactual does not generally aggregate to the national synthetic counterfactual because weights and donor support differ across groups. A weighted sum of supported group effects is therefore reported only as a contribution for that covered sample, never substituted for the national headline.
