\label{sec:results}

\Cref{tab:main} summarizes the estimates for the primary outcome from the three designs. The synthetic control is the primary causal specification; the international difference-in-differences and the regression discontinuity in time are complementary analyses of the same shock. The subsections below present each design's main figure and table.

\subsection{Synthetic control: main effect on daily productive activity}
\label{sec:main_results}

The synthetic-control estimate shows a collapse in observable GitHub activity (\cref{fig:sc,tab:sc_main}). Bangladesh's active-user rate averages 2.021 percent during July 18--23, compared with a synthetic counterfactual of 15.277 percent. The ATT is $-$13.256 percentage points, equivalent to an 86.8 percent reduction relative to the synthetic counterfactual. The pre-treatment RMSPE is 3.260 percentage points and the post/pre RMSPE ratio is 4.192.

Keeping the original donor weights, the complete low-connectivity days July 19--22 yield actual activity of 0.212 percent, a 14.687 percent synthetic counterfactual, and a gap of $-$14.475 percentage points ($-$98.55 percent), alongside the primary July 18--23 estimate of $-$13.256 percentage points ($-$86.77 percent). The narrower window is a reported sensitivity, not a replacement for the primary estimand. Its residual activity averages 21.75 accounts per day. If every such active account were outside Bangladesh, location error among those observed active would account for at most 0.212 percent of the eligible cohort per day. This is not an upper bound on all incorrectly located accounts: inactive accounts' physical locations remain unobserved, and exceptional connectivity or automated publication could also explain residual activity. The case audit in \cref{sec:automation_audit} directly documents automation in part of this residual.


The synthetic control places 47.6 percent weight on Pakistan, 32.5 percent on Ghana, and 19.9 percent on Kenya (\cref{tab:sc_main}). No other donor receives positive weight in the baseline optimization. After restoration, shown descriptively in \cref{fig:sc}, the average weekly gap between Bangladesh and its synthetic counterpart narrows to about $-$3 percentage points in the first two weeks ($-$3.11 and $-$2.89 percentage points) and turns positive in the third week; the positive average gaps in weeks $+4$ and $+5$ (4.24 and 4.67 percentage points) lie within the range of pre-treatment weekly gaps and are not interpreted as effects of the shutdown or of restoration. Bangladesh is more extreme than all fourteen in-space placebos in both absolute ATT and RMSPE-ratio comparisons, yielding rank statistics of 0.0667, the smallest value attainable with fourteen placebo units (\cref{fig:placebo,tab:sc_placebos}).

The estimate changes little when donors are removed one at a time (\cref{tab:loo} in \cref{sec:sc_diagnostics}): the ATT ranges from $-$13.256 to $-$12.886 percentage points. In particular, excluding Pakistan produces $-$12.905 percentage points, excluding Ghana produces $-$13.209 percentage points, and excluding Kenya produces $-$12.886 percentage points. This matters because both Pakistan and Kenya experienced localized connectivity disruptions near the broader pre-treatment period: Internet Society reports regional restrictions in Pakistan around July 14--17 \citep{isoc2024pakbal,isoc2024pakpun}, while Cloudflare documents a major Kenyan traffic disruption on June 25 \citep{cloudflare2024}.
The limited movement in the treatment estimate when either donor is removed reduces, though does not eliminate, concern that these donor-specific episodes drive the result.

\paragraph{Cumulative shortfall and persistence.}
Using the unchanged synthetic weights and 10,241-account cohort, the July 18--23 gap sums to 8,145 active user-days. The first daily return to the synthetic level is July 27, by which point the cumulative deficit is 10,186 user-days. A daily crossing is not sustained recovery: the first nonnegative trailing seven-day mean gap is August 13 (cumulative deficit 12,068); the first seven consecutive nonnegative daily gaps end August 16. Returning to Bangladesh's own pre-period mean is a different criterion: the first daily crossing is August 14 and the first seven-day mean crossing August 22.

Through September 4, negative daily gaps total 13,526 user-days and positive gaps total 9,893, leaving a net deficit of 3,633. The cumulative deficit peaks at 13,237 on August 9 and is never fully offset within observation. Positive post-restoration gaps indicate an arithmetic offset, but cannot establish causal catch-up: early-July pre-treatment elevation, later domestic events, and donor disturbances also affect this counterfactual. Fifteen of 43 post-window days remain below it. These are descriptive sums of the original gap, not losses in hours worked or total software production; persistence after September 4 is not observed in this national panel.

\subsection{Difference-in-differences and event study}
\label{sec:did_results}

The international DiD estimate is $-$13.088 percentage points, nearly identical to the synthetic-control ATT (\cref{tab:did}, Panel A). Removing national holidays yields $-$13.379 percentage points. The agreement is informative because the two methods weight donors differently: synthetic control chooses donor weights to reproduce Bangladesh before the shutdown, whereas the international DiD gives equal weight to donor-country changes. Bangladesh's estimate is also more extreme than that of every in-space placebo, whose estimates range from $-$1.72 to 1.60 percentage points; as for the synthetic control, the resulting rank value of 0.0667 is the minimum attainable with fifteen countries.

\input{tables/tab_did}

\Cref{fig:did_es} reports the weekly event study from \cref{eq:es_agg}. Its shutdown-week coefficient, $-$13.09 percentage points, reproduces the aggregate DiD estimate and is more than twice as large in absolute value as any pre-shutdown weekly coefficient. The pre-shutdown period, however, is volatile and does not display a clean conventional parallel-trends pattern. Weekly coefficients range from $-$4.89 percentage points (week $-5$, June 13--19) to 6.10 (week $-2$, July 4--10), and Bangladesh was above its pre-period position relative to the donors in the two weeks immediately before the shutdown. This mirrors the imperfect early-July fit of the synthetic control (\cref{fig:sc}). Because the reference is the full pre-period mean, this elevated activity does not generate the shutdown estimate; measured against the immediately preceding week alone, the decline would be larger. Given this pre-period volatility, the weekly coefficients are not interpreted as an identified dynamic causal treatment path. They are diagnostic evidence that the largest break in the series is concentrated in the shutdown week.

The donor-placebo reference intervals in \cref{fig:did_es} should be read with care. They are not conventional sampling confidence intervals: they scale the spread of the fourteen donor placebo estimates in each week. Bangladesh's pre-shutdown weekly coefficients have a standard deviation of 3.04 percentage points, compared with 0.70 to 2.18 percentage points for the donors treated as placebo units. Several pre-period coefficients for Bangladesh are therefore as extreme as any placebo, and the reference intervals are not fully calibrated measures of uncertainty for Bangladesh. The more informative comparison is with Bangladesh's own pre-shutdown range: the shutdown coefficient lies far outside it, whereas every post-restoration coefficient lies within it.

After restoration, the gap narrows quickly. The coefficients are $-$2.66 and $-$2.76 percentage points in the two weeks after the shutdown and are positive from week $+3$ onward. The positive coefficients in weeks $+4$ to $+6$ (2.53 to 4.84 percentage points) lie within the range of pre-shutdown fluctuations and should not be interpreted as gains caused by the shutdown. Recovery dynamics within Bangladesh are examined by repository sector in \cref{sec:sector_results}.

\subsection{Productive, shipping, and collaboration outcomes}
\label{sec:secondary}

The collapse is not limited to a generic activity indicator. Holding the primary synthetic-control weights fixed, productive activity falls by 11.665 percentage points relative to a 13.469 percent counterfactual, or 86.6 percent. Shipping activity falls by 0.979 percentage points relative to a 1.049 percent counterfactual, or 93.3 percent. Collaboration activity falls by 0.611 percentage points relative to a 0.654 percent counterfactual, or 93.5 percent. Count outcomes show similarly large relative declines in collaboration events and shipping output (\cref{tab:secondary}). International DiD estimates for the same outcomes, which point in the same direction, are reported in \cref{tab:did_secondary}.

Allowing the donor weights to re-optimize separately for each outcome produces comparable magnitudes: $-$86.7 percent for productive activity, $-$90.5 percent for shipping activity, and $-$91.9 percent for collaboration activity. The in-space placebo ATT rank is again 0.0667 for all three key activity-rate outcomes. The RMSPE-ratio rank is 0.0667 for productive and shipping activity but 0.1333 for collaboration activity. Among the count outcomes, shipping output has a rank of 0.0667 on both statistics, whereas collaboration events have an ATT rank of 0.2667 and an RMSPE-ratio rank of 0.1333, so Bangladesh is not the most extreme unit for that outcome. I therefore treat collaboration as consistent secondary evidence rather than giving it the same inferential weight as the primary and shipping outcomes.
