\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

\subsection{Main effect on daily productive activity}
\label{sec:main_results}

The synthetic-control estimate shows an exceptionally large collapse in observable GitHub activity (\cref{fig:sc,tab:main}). Bangladesh's active-user rate averages 2.021 percent during July 18--23, compared with a synthetic counterfactual of 15.277 percent. The ATT is $-$13.256 percentage points, equivalent to an 86.8 percent reduction relative to the synthetic counterfactual. The pre-treatment RMSPE is 3.260 percentage points and the post/pre RMSPE ratio is 4.192.

The synthetic control places 47.6 percent weight on Pakistan, 32.5 percent on Ghana, and 19.9 percent on Kenya (\cref{tab:weights}). No other donor receives positive weight in the baseline optimization. Bangladesh is more extreme than all fourteen in-space placebos in both absolute ATT and RMSPE-ratio comparisons, yielding rank statistics of 0.0667, the smallest value attainable with fourteen placebo units (\cref{fig:placebo,tab:sc_placebos}).

The leave-one-out exercise is highly stable (\cref{tab:loo}). Across specifications that remove one donor at a time, the ATT ranges only from $-$13.256 to $-$12.886 percentage points. In particular, excluding Pakistan produces $-$12.905 percentage points, excluding Ghana produces $-$13.209 percentage points, and excluding Kenya produces $-$12.886 percentage points. This matters because both Pakistan and Kenya experienced localized connectivity disruptions near the broader pre-treatment period: Internet Society reports regional restrictions in Pakistan around July 14--17, while Cloudflare documents a major Kenyan traffic disruption on June 25 \citep{isoc2024,cloudflare2024}.

\subsection{Difference-in-differences and temporal robustness}
\label{sec:did_results}

The international DiD estimate is $-$13.088 percentage points, nearly identical to the synthetic-control ATT. Removing national holidays yields $-$13.379 percentage points. The agreement is notable because the two methods use different weighting logic: synthetic control chooses donor weights to reproduce Bangladesh before the shutdown, whereas the reported international DiD gives equal weight to donor-country changes.

The RDiT produces a smaller point estimate but the same qualitative conclusion. A local linear specification at the July 17 cutoff estimates a discontinuity of $-$9.28 percentage points in Bangladesh's active-user rate; a controlled Bangladesh-minus-Philippines version yields $-$9.16 percentage points. HAC $p$-values are approximately 0.065 in both cases, while a Philippines geographic placebo is near zero (\cref{tab:rdit}).

Auxiliary event-study specifications show a decline around the shutdown, but because the archived event-study implementation is not fully documented, I do not use those estimates as primary evidence.

\subsection{Productive, shipping, and collaboration outcomes}
\label{sec:secondary}

The collapse is not limited to a generic activity indicator. Holding the primary synthetic-control weights fixed, productive activity falls by 15.179 percentage points relative to a 17.555 percent counterfactual, or 86.5 percent. Shipping activity falls by 1.333 percentage points relative to a 1.415 percent counterfactual, or 94.2 percent. Collaboration activity falls by 0.780 percentage points relative to a 0.834 percent counterfactual, or 93.6 percent. Count outcomes show similarly large relative declines in collaboration events and shipping output (\cref{tab:secondary}).

Allowing the donor weights to re-optimize separately for each outcome produces comparable magnitudes: $-$86.2 percent for productive activity, $-$92.2 percent for shipping activity, and $-$92.1 percent for collaboration activity. The in-space placebo ATT rank is again 0.0667 for all three key activity-rate outcomes. The RMSPE-ratio rank is 0.0667 for productive and shipping activity but 0.20 for collaboration activity. I therefore treat collaboration as consistent secondary evidence rather than giving it the same inferential weight as the primary and shipping outcomes.
