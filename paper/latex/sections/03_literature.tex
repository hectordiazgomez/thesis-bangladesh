\section{Related Literature}
\label{sec:literature}

A first literature estimates the economic consequences of Internet access and Internet disruptions. \citet{hjort2019} exploit the arrival of submarine Internet cables in Africa and find increases in employment, especially in higher-skill occupations, together with evidence of firm entry, productivity, and exporting. This literature establishes connectivity as a productive infrastructure input rather than merely a consumption amenity. The shutdown literature studies the reverse shock. \citet*{tagat2024} develop an econometric framework for measuring shutdown costs across countries and emphasize impacts on output, employment, and investment. \citet{gunsilius2023} estimate large reductions in economic activity from an Internet shutdown in India using a discontinuity-based method. These studies motivate the hypothesis that intentional connectivity losses can generate immediate real economic costs.

A second literature uses digital trace data to measure knowledge work. GitHub is especially useful because public events are timestamped and tied to concrete production and collaboration tasks. \citet{wachs2022} use GitHub to map the geography of open-source software production. \citet{hu2023} study how pandemic lockdowns affected open-source contributions in China and show that the direction of the effect can depend on context and adaptation. \citet{kreitmeir2024} compile daily GitHub outcomes to study Italy's temporary ban on ChatGPT. Their design is particularly close methodologically: because user-day GitHub actions are sparse and count distributions are highly skewed, they emphasize binary daily indicators, fixed effects, event studies, and robustness to outliers. I follow that logic by making the daily active-user rate the primary outcome and treating raw counts as secondary intensive-margin outcomes.

A third literature concerns comparative-case causal inference. Synthetic control methods construct a weighted combination of untreated units that approximates the treated unit before an intervention \citep*{abadie2010}. \citet{abadie2021} emphasizes transparent donor selection, pre-treatment fit, and placebo or leave-one-out exercises as central diagnostics. Synthetic difference-in-differences combines reweighting of units and time periods with difference-in-differences structure \citep{arkhangelsky2021}. I use conventional synthetic control as the headline specification because its weights and pre-fit are transparent in this application, while SDID is treated as a robustness exercise. Finally, I use regression discontinuity in time as an additional temporal check, following the cautions in \citet{hausman2018} regarding serial correlation, functional form, and the conceptual differences between time-based and cross-sectional RD designs.
