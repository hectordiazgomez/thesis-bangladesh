\section{Robustness, Identification Threats, and Limitations}
\label{sec:robustness}

\subsection{Synthetic-control diagnostics}
\label{sec:sc_diagnostics}

The primary synthetic control improves pre-treatment fit relative to using the Philippines alone, but the fit is not perfect. The pre-treatment RMSPE is 3.26 percentage points, and Bangladesh exhibits temporary deviations from the synthetic series in early July (\cref{fig:sc}). The international event study shows the same pattern: Bangladesh's pre-shutdown weekly coefficients relative to the donor mean range from $-$4.89 to 6.10 percentage points and are more volatile than those of any donor (\cref{fig:did_es}). The paper therefore reports pre-fit explicitly rather than treating the synthetic control as a perfect replica. The unusually large post-treatment gap and the stability of the estimate under leave-one-out exercises are the main reasons the design remains informative.

In-time placebos ask whether Bangladesh's own series produces large gaps in ordinary weeks. Under a rule fixed in advance---six-day pseudo-treatment windows, non-overlapping and moving backward from July 17, each with at least four weeks of fitting data and with donor weights re-estimated using only earlier days---there are eight eligible pseudo-windows (\cref{fig:sc_intime,tab:sc_intime}). The actual shutdown ATT of $-$13.256 percentage points is the largest absolute gap among the nine windows. The largest pseudo-gap is $+$7.349 percentage points (July 6--11), so the actual ATT is about 1.8 times as large in magnitude and has the opposite sign. The actual window also ranks first by the RMSPE ratio. Because the pseudo-windows differ in fit length and are not exchangeable, these ranks are descriptive rather than a formal $p$-value. The three early-July pseudo-windows are all positive, consistent with the elevated pre-shutdown Bangladesh activity visible in the event study (\cref{fig:did_es}).

The same early-July elevation explains why a synthetic difference-in-differences estimator \citep{arkhangelsky2021} yields a larger estimate, $-$17.9495 percentage points (\cref{tab:sdid}). The difference comes from the time weights rather than the donor weights: SDID places 72 percent of its time-weight mass on July 1--17, including 27.1 percent on July 2 and 30.2 percent on July 10, so its weighted Bangladesh baseline is 22.54 percent, compared with a uniform pre-period mean of 17.25 percent; the synthetic control and the DiD instead weight all pre-treatment days equally. The comparison therefore shows how sensitive the estimated magnitude is to whether the baseline emphasizes the final pre-shutdown weeks or the full pre-period; it does not indicate that either estimator is invalid. The synthetic control remains the primary specification.

The donor pool also contains countries with their own short-lived connectivity disturbances. Pakistan experienced regional restrictions around July 14--17, immediately before Bangladesh's national shutdown, and Kenya experienced a major traffic disruption on June 25. These episodes are a reason to avoid presenting donor selection as mechanically pristine. Still, removing Pakistan or Kenya individually changes the ATT by less than 0.4 percentage points (\cref{tab:loo}).

\subsection{Metadata and measurement}
\label{sec:measurement}

Commit metadata resolution is similar across periods: 82.34 percent of the pre-period target commits are found, compared with 84.24 percent during the shutdown and 81.81 percent after (\cref{tab:metadata}). The shutdown mechanism is therefore not generated by a sharp deterioration in commit-object availability during the treatment window.

A related limitation concerns push-listing completeness: although 99.5--99.8 percent of pushes in every period expose a complete commit list, the share of nominal commits captured within complete pushes falls from 90.27 percent before the shutdown to 73.11 percent during the shutdown and 71.45 percent afterward (\cref{tab:push_completeness}). This occurs because a small number of large pushes account for a disproportionate share of commits and are more likely to have incomplete commit listings. Since the strict mechanism sample restricts to complete pushes, this does not mechanically invalidate the 28.27 percent backlog estimate, but it does imply uneven commit-level coverage across periods. The lower coverage during and after the shutdown is therefore a measurement limitation that should be considered when interpreting the mechanism results.

Location is another measurement limitation. GitHub location fields are user-entered and may be missing, stale, or imprecise. The estimates should be read as applying to accounts associated with Bangladesh and the donor countries, not as a verified census of developers physically located in each country on every date. Public-event data additionally exclude private repositories. If private and public work respond differently to a shutdown, the paper estimates the effect on the observable public margin rather than on all software production.

\subsection{Sector classification and the sectoral recovery analysis}
\label{sec:sector_robustness}

\Cref{tab:sector_threshold} repeats the sectoral recovery analysis keeping only repositories whose classifier confidence is at least 0.70 or 0.90 instead of 0.80. The qualitative ordering of the main sectors is stable. At all three thresholds, Finance and Information begin their first run of two consecutive weeks at or above 80 in week $+3$; Education and Health do so between weeks $+5$ and $+8$; and Accommodation/Food never does, with week $+8$ indices between 60.3 and 64.9. Real Estate's late recovery is broadly but not fully robust: its week $+8$ index is 90.0 and 85.5 at the 0.70 and 0.80 thresholds but 75.5 at 0.90. Retail's week $+4$ overshoot appears at every threshold (indices between 153.7 and 159.1), but the start of its first two-week run at or above 80 varies from week $+4$ (0.70) to week $+9$ (0.80) and week $+7$ (0.90). I therefore do not emphasize a precise recovery date for Retail.

Three sectors with substantial activity are excluded from the main results. Professional/\allowbreak Scientific and Administrative Support have 577 and 196 sampled users active in the baseline period at the 0.80 threshold, but only 125 and 48 at 0.90, so their indices depend heavily on the threshold. In Arts, Entertainment, and Recreation, a single repository accounts for 82.5 percent of pre-shutdown events among the sector's classified repositories, so a sector index would largely track one project.

The classification has further limitations. First, the sector describes the application domain inferred from a repository's text, not the employer or industry of the developer: a developer employed in banking may work on a general-purpose library, and a student exercise may be classified as Retail because it implements an online shop. Second, classifier errors are possible, and this paper does not validate the classifier's accuracy on Bangladeshi repositories. The model card reports that the training labels are GPT-4.1 judgements rather than human annotations and that, on public GitHub repositories, roughly half of what the model labels confidently arguably belongs to no sector at all, such as coursework, portfolios, exercises, and generic tooling \citep{quispe2026naics}; the score threshold mitigates but does not eliminate this. Third, coverage is incomplete. Only 55.1 percent of repositories have a recoverable historical README, and activity in unclassified repositories does not enter any sector. If newly created repositories are less likely than established ones to have a README at their first observed activity date, post-shutdown recovery through new projects would be understated. Fourth, a user may contribute to several sectors, so sector indices are not shares of a fixed workforce. Finally, the sectoral confidence intervals do not adjust for the many sector-week comparisons reported, and they reflect sampling variation across users rather than common shocks.

\subsection{Concurrent unrest and causal interpretation}
\label{sec:unrest}

The most important identification threat is that the Internet shutdown was itself a response to political unrest. Protests, curfews, transportation disruptions, workplace closures, fear, or other domestic conditions could directly reduce software work even if connectivity were available. Comparisons with donor countries, whether through synthetic-control weights or the equal-weighted DiD, cannot absorb shocks unique to Bangladesh that coincide with the shutdown. For this reason, the headline estimate should be interpreted as the effect of the shutdown episode under the identifying assumption that the abrupt Bangladesh-specific deviation is primarily attributable to lost connectivity.

Several findings make the connectivity interpretation more plausible without fully proving it. First, the break is tightly aligned with the network outage. Second, activity begins to recover as connectivity returns (\cref{fig:did_es,fig:sector_index}). Third, downstream online tasks such as shipping and collaboration fall at least as strongly as the general activity rate. Fourth, commit metadata reveal a large stock of code authored during the outage but pushed immediately after restoration. A pure `developers stopped working because of unrest' story does not naturally predict that last pattern. Nevertheless, the mechanism evidence is complementary, not a substitute for randomized assignment.
