\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Bangladesh's July 2024 Internet shutdown provides a high-frequency case study of what happens to digitally mediated production when national connectivity is abruptly removed. Using public GitHub events for 59,158 pre-active accounts across fifteen countries, the paper finds that Bangladesh's daily productive-activity rate fell by 13.26 percentage points relative to a synthetic counterfactual during July 18--23, an 86.8 percent decline. International difference-in-differences, secondary outcomes, placebos, and temporal discontinuity analyses point in the same direction (\cref{tab:main}). The cumulative synthetic-control deficit is 8,145 active user-days in the shutdown window and 3,633 by September 4 after partial offsets. A first daily crossing on July 27 is weaker evidence of recovery than the first nonnegative seven-day mean gap on August 13. These descriptive sums inherit imperfect pre-fit and concurrent-shock limitations.

The commit-level evidence shows that the observed backlog was small relative to normal creation. After the approximately 15:00 UTC July 18 onset, 1,097 commits were authored and subsequently observed publicly by August 10, a 96.35 percent fall against duration-matched pre-shutdown windows (96.75 percent against the original six-day windows). That cohort's 752 delayed commits equal 2.50 percent of normal volume. Because later pushes and unpublished code are unobserved, these figures do not show how much of the shortfall was work not done rather than work published after August 10 or never published. The original calendar-cohort backlog share of 28.27 percent and onset-cohort share of 68.55 percent describe delayed publication among the surviving observed commits; neither establishes that total software work continued at a comparable rate.

The international extension provides separate synthetic comparisons for fixed repository-domain cohorts rather than treating a national before--after recovery index as a sector-specific causal effect. Its interpretation is conditional on common measurement and counterfactual stability; it does not identify effects on industry value added or developers' employers. The 40 accounts that remain publicly active on July 19--22 are not interchangeable with the 250 users in the strict onset-author cohort. Direct automation evidence in 113 of their 180 pushes shows that residual posting includes machine-generated publication. Excluding only those pushes lowers the residual daily activity rate from 0.212 to 0.117 percent; the remainder still cannot be equated with verified human work inside Bangladesh.

A within-Bangladesh analysis of the restoration period adds a further qualification: reconnection did not immediately restore observable activity. Recovery was faster in Finance- and Information-related repositories than in Education- and Health-related repositories, and activity in Accommodation/Food-related repositories remained depressed throughout the observed window. These sectoral patterns are descriptive comparisons with each sector's own pre-shutdown level rather than causal estimates against an untreated control.

The paper's main limitation is the coincidence of the shutdown with severe domestic unrest. Future work could combine network telemetry at subnational or provider level with developer location at finer geographic resolution, or exploit variation in restoration timing across networks and regions. Such designs could more sharply separate the direct effect of connectivity from the broader social environment. Even with this limitation, the evidence is consistent with observable software-development activity being highly exposed to network availability and with public development platforms providing unusually granular measures of that exposure.
